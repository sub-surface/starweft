# STARWEFT Progression Architecture: Research, Aptitudes and Milestones

Status: implementation specification and systems review

Related requirement families: `PROG`, `YOU`, `UX`, `A11Y`, `SAVE`, `TYPE`

## Hard constraints

- Preserve zero dependencies, zero build and direct `file://` play.
- Research and aptitude points remain distinct resources with distinct reasons to exist.
- Consolidation means one coherent progression home, not collapsing every system into one currency or one undifferentiated tree.
- Progression must unlock decisions, verbs, scale or identity; avoid piles of minor passive modifiers.
- The presentation must remain responsive and keyboard-operable without a framework.

---

# 1. Executive assessment

STARWEFT currently has three related but weakly composed progression systems:

1. **Research** generated primarily by prosperous populations and spent on a branched network technology tree.
2. **Aptitudes** bought with points granted by milestones and intended to grow the captain rather than the network.
3. **Milestones** checked periodically, granting one aptitude point when a threshold is crossed.

The underlying distinction is good:

- research says what the network has learned and can build;
- aptitudes say what kind of operator the player has become;
- milestones say what the player has actually done.

The current experience does not communicate that distinction strongly enough.

Observed problems:

- the compact tech canvas duplicates the expanded tree without being a useful decision surface;
- the expanded tree combines a pannable canvas, detail panel and long list, creating competing representations;
- technology nodes, doctrines, aptitudes and milestones live in different places despite forming one progression story;
- research points and aptitude points are not presented together as related but distinct primitives;
- aptitude rows wrap badly and depend on narrow inline layouts;
- milestones are a flat completed/not-completed list, with little progress visibility or ceremony;
- the tech tree can feel like a collection of unlocks rather than an authored arc from one probe to an autonomous galactic network;
- doctrine is mechanically attached to research but narratively closer to identity and stance;
- the player cannot easily answer "what can I work toward next?" across both systems.

The target is a single **Development** home that presents two currencies and three layers of progression:

```text
WORLD KNOWLEDGE       PLAYER MASTERY       PROOF
Research points   +   Aptitude points  <-  Milestones
      |                    |
Network research       Captain aptitudes
      \                    /
       Doctrine, stance and run identity
```

The systems remain distinct, but are designed and displayed as one coherent answer to:

> What has the weave learned, what have I become, and what should I attempt next?

# 2. Semantic contract

## 2.1 Research

Research represents knowledge generated by a functioning civilisation and recovered from exploration.

Sources:

- prosperous population systems;
- cartography data sales;
- wonders and anomalies;
- rare event discoveries;
- rescued specialists or archives;
- specific endgame projects.

Research purchases:

- new commands;
- automation scale;
- hull/facility capability;
- network infrastructure;
- economic or cartographic analysis;
- Scourge understanding;
- endgame production capability.

Research should normally change **what the network can do**.

## 2.2 Aptitude points

Aptitude points represent mastery earned through performed deeds.

Sources:

- milestones;
- rare character-defining decisions;
- major run transitions where explicitly specified.

Aptitudes purchase:

- operational preferences;
- player-specific efficiencies;
- risk tolerances;
- information advantages;
- manual-control affordances;
- small rule-bending capabilities tied to demonstrated play.

Aptitudes should normally change **how the player does things**, not unlock basic content the network requires.

## 2.3 Milestones

Milestones are evidence of play. They should:

- recognise meaningful achievements;
- teach breadth without becoming a checklist treadmill;
- expose progress before completion where possible;
- grant aptitude points or identity recognition;
- contribute to Chronicle and post-run history;
- occasionally unlock cosmetic titles, sigil marks or narrative recognition.

Milestones should not be hidden achievements with arbitrary conditions unless secrecy is part of the delight.

## 2.4 Doctrine

Doctrine is a run-defining commitment between network capability and player identity.

It should not feel like an ordinary technology node.

Doctrine answers:

> What does this weave believe it is for?

It may:

- alter research costs or branch emphasis;
- change faction response;
- bias milestone opportunities;
- modify available crisis policies;
- influence stance/endgame transitions.

Doctrine belongs visually between Research and Aptitudes, with explicit one-per-run permanence.

## 2.5 Stance

Scourge stance is a historical commitment, not a progression currency purchase.

It belongs in the same Development/Identity home for explanation and consequences, but remains triggered by world state and choice rather than bought with points.

# 3. Progression design rules

## 3.1 Every research node must justify itself

A node should do at least one:

- unlock a first-class verb;
- unlock a new automation scale;
- open a new geography;
- create a new strategic trade-off;
- enable a new hull/facility role;
- reveal meaningful information;
- establish an endgame capability.

Nodes that only add a small percentage should be:

- folded into a meaningful parent;
- moved to an aptitude if personal;
- made part of a facility/hull upgrade;
- removed.

## 3.2 Aptitudes must not duplicate research

Bad overlap:

- research unlocks faster routes;
- aptitude separately gives generic route speed +5%;
- origin also gives generic route speed +5%.

Better separation:

- research unlocks route automation or gate technology;
- aptitude changes manual rerouting, risk policy or information quality;
- origin changes starting conditions or early identity.

## 3.3 Progression should mirror the player journey

Recommended research eras:

### Era I -- Thread

- FETCH and clear command expression;
- basic routes;
- first facilities;
- survey and map literacy;
- relay network.

### Era II -- Weave

- directives;
- richer market analysis;
- specialised hulls;
- passenger/logistics professions;
- regional operations;
- advanced facilities.

### Era III -- Wake

- crisis response;
- escort/patrol;
- bastion and evacuation infrastructure;
- rival diplomacy/privateering;
- doctrine crystallisation.

### Era IV -- History

- Weftworks and auto-yards;
- Deep Drives;
- Panacea chain;
- fortress network;
- colony arks;
- stance-specific capstones.

The player should see the tree telling the same story as the campaign.

## 3.4 Branches should represent strategic questions

Current branch names may remain, but each needs a plain-language promise.

Example:

- **Logistics** -- move more reliably and automate reserves;
- **Core** -- grow infrastructure, industry and settlement capacity;
- **Frontier** -- see farther, travel deeper and profit from discovery;
- **Vanguard** -- protect, escort, interdict and operate under threat;
- **Scourge** -- understand, resist and ultimately answer extinction.

If a node does not belong clearly, the branch model or node is wrong.

## 3.5 Prerequisites must be meaningful

Edges should express conceptual dependency, not merely force tree shape.

Examples:

- directives require route understanding;
- convoy escort requires patrol or combat awareness;
- evacuation transports require passenger foundations;
- Panacea production requires prior Scourge analysis;
- auto-yards require mature route automation.

Avoid cross-branch prerequisite tangles that make the tree unreadable.

# 4. Aptitude architecture

## 4.1 Disciplines

Use a small number of disciplines with clear player identities.

Suggested disciplines:

### Weaver

Orchestration, route diagnosis, command control and automation.

### Wayfinder

Exploration, path choice, data recovery and frontier survival.

### Steward

Population, prosperity, relief, construction and diplomacy.

### Warden

Escort, patrol, defence, crisis response and controlled aggression.

Names may retain existing categories if they are already strong, but the responsibilities should be this clear.

## 4.2 Chain shape

Each discipline should have:

- 3-5 foundational choices;
- one or two mutually exclusive forks;
- a capstone earned late enough to feel authored;
- no requirement to buy filler nodes solely to reach the interesting choice.

Aptitudes should be fewer and richer than technologies.

## 4.3 Effect standards

Good aptitude effects:

- preview one additional route consequence;
- allow one emergency queue insertion without dropping assignment;
- negotiate a haven open once per crisis;
- retain a fraction of unsold cartography data after destruction;
- see uncertain Scourge pressure more clearly;
- convert a failed rescue deadline into partial credit;
- reserve one ship from employ-idle/auto-yard automation;
- improve manual convoy coordination.

Weak effects:

- +2% to all prices;
- +3% to every ship speed;
- invisible generic multipliers with no interaction.

Numerical effects can support a clear behavioral effect but should not be the entire identity.

## 4.4 Respec and permanence

Recommended policy:

- ordinary aptitudes may be respecced at Home for a meaningful but non-punitive cost;
- doctrine remains permanent per run;
- stance remains permanent or changes only through an explicit major story event;
- respec history is journaled;
- no respec is available during an unresolved modal/crisis decision if it would invalidate the choice.

The final policy must be stated in the UI before purchase.

# 5. Milestones

## 5.1 Expand milestone coverage

Add milestones across different play styles.

### Logistics

- first meaningful delivery;
- first complete route loop;
- maintain a reserve for a duration;
- move a large lifetime cargo total;
- restore a blocked industry;
- operate a multi-stage chain;
- maintain several healthy routes simultaneously.

### Exploration

- first survey;
- first unsold data return;
- first wonder record;
- chart a region;
- return from the Badlands;
- sell diverse data types;
- discover a rare body or alien trace.

### Stewardship

- raise a system from deprivation to prosperity;
- complete a relief project;
- resettle a cohort;
- support a bastion;
- integrate refugees without collapse;
- build a frontier settlement.

### Fleet and command

- operate a fleet size threshold;
- maintain no stranded ships for a period;
- complete a passenger itinerary;
- save a convoy;
- resolve a blocked route;
- use directives across several regions.

### Conflict

- break a blockade;
- escort a rival convoy;
- survive an ambush;
- win without high infamy;
- complete a privateering path where chosen.

### History

- choose doctrine;
- choose stance;
- save a threatened system;
- lose and later rebuild a system;
- complete each ending;
- create a distinctive Chronicle outcome.

## 5.2 Progressive milestones

Some milestones should have tiers rather than separate arbitrary duplicates:

```text
Threadbearer I   100 cargo moved
Threadbearer II  1,000 cargo moved
Threadbearer III 10,000 cargo moved
```

Not every tier must grant an aptitude point. Later tiers may grant titles, sigil marks, Chronicle recognition or small prestige rewards.

## 5.3 Progress visibility

Each incomplete milestone should show:

- current value;
- target;
- concise description;
- reward;
- whether it is available in the current mode/difficulty;
- related action or surface.

Hidden milestones should be explicitly marked as hidden rather than appearing absent.

## 5.4 Avoid exploit incentives

Milestone tests should favour meaningful outcomes over repetitive micro-actions.

Examples:

- count useful delivered units or reserve coverage, not buy/sell clicks;
- count complete route loops, not route creation/deletion;
- count distinct restored systems, not oscillating one system across a threshold;
- count civilians arriving safely, not repeated boarding cancellation.

# 6. One Development home

## 6.1 Remove the mini tech tree

The dock-level miniature canvas should be removed.

Replace it with one compact launcher:

```text
[ tree icon ]  RESEARCH 240   APTITUDE 2
                3 available   milestone 72%
```

The launcher may use a small icon and badges. It should not attempt to reproduce the full tree.

## 6.2 Full Development surface

Open a large responsive surface with a stable header:

```text
DEVELOPMENT
Research: 240 ◇     Aptitude: 2 ◆
Doctrine: Wayfarer  Stance: undecided

[ NETWORK RESEARCH ] [ CAPTAIN APTITUDES ] [ MILESTONES ] [ IDENTITY ]
```

The two currencies remain visible across tabs so their relationship is always clear.

## 6.3 Network Research view

Recommended structure:

- branch filter/legend;
- responsive tree/map region;
- selected-node detail;
- available recommendations;
- owned history/capstones;
- plain-language dependency path.

Replace the current competing canvas + full list duplication with one primary representation.

Preferred implementation:

- semantic DOM nodes in a CSS grid;
- native SVG edge overlay where useful;
- branch columns on wide screens;
- tier/branch cards on narrow screens;
- keyboard navigation between nodes;
- no free-pan requirement for basic use;
- optional zoom only if the tree genuinely exceeds the viewport.

Canvas may remain temporarily during migration, but the target should be accessible DOM-first nodes rather than a canvas-only decision surface.

## 6.4 Aptitude view

Use discipline columns/cards with:

- point total;
- owned/available/locked state;
- prerequisite path;
- effect explanation;
- permanence/respec notice;
- preview of downstream choices.

Do not place many inline buttons in one narrow row. Every aptitude card should have a predictable minimum width and stack cleanly.

## 6.5 Milestone view

Organise by category and progress:

- close to completion;
- recently completed;
- logistics;
- exploration;
- stewardship;
- fleet;
- conflict;
- history.

A recently completed milestone can produce a small persistent highlight rather than only a disappearing toast.

## 6.6 Identity view

Owns:

- sigil and name;
- origin;
- doctrine;
- stance;
- titles/cosmetic marks;
- high-level service record;
- Chronicle link.

Do not duplicate the full aptitude and technology interfaces here; provide summary and links.

# 7. Recommendations and legibility

## 7.1 Show why a point exists

Research header:

> Generated by prosperous populations and sold discoveries. Spent on network capability.

Aptitude header:

> Earned through milestones. Spent on how you command the weave.

## 7.2 Suggested next choices

Offer 2-4 recommendations derived from current state:

- a currently blocking capability;
- a chosen play-style continuation;
- a Scourge readiness need;
- an affordable useful node.

Recommendations must explain their basis and never auto-purchase.

## 7.3 Consequence preview

Every node/card should state:

- what new action or capability appears;
- where it appears in the UI;
- relevant dependencies;
- save/permanence consequences;
- branch/doctrine synergy;
- whether it affects current ships, future ships or both.

## 7.4 No misleading tree status

Distinguish:

- hidden by story;
- visible but prerequisites missing;
- available but unaffordable;
- affordable;
- owned;
- mutually excluded;
- stance-locked;
- obsolete/superseded if migration requires it.

# 8. Data architecture

## 8.1 Technology definitions

Extend or audit `D.TECHS` fields:

```js
{
  id,
  name,
  branch,
  era,
  tier,
  cost,
  req,
  excludes,
  visibleIf,
  desc,
  outcome,
  uiHome,
  tags
}
```

`outcome` should describe the player-facing capability separately from flavour text.

## 8.2 Aptitude definitions

```js
{
  id,
  name,
  discipline,
  tier,
  cost: 1,
  req,
  excludes,
  desc,
  outcome,
  respecPolicy,
  tags
}
```

## 8.3 Milestone definitions

```js
{
  id,
  category,
  label,
  description,
  target,
  progress(state),
  complete(state),
  reward,
  tier,
  visibleIf,
  repeatable: false
}
```

Avoid arbitrary `try/catch` around milestone logic as the long-term contract. Validators should catch invalid definitions before play.

## 8.4 Derived progression report

Add a DOM-free report:

```js
SW.progression.buildReport(state)
```

It returns:

- currency totals and recent sources;
- available/affordable research;
- aptitude choices;
- milestone progress;
- doctrine/stance summary;
- recommendations;
- warnings about locked/excluded choices.

The UI renders the report rather than separately re-deriving policy.

# 9. Balance

## 9.1 Research pacing

Measure:

- research earned by source;
- time/tick to first node in each era;
- stalled periods with no meaningful affordable choice;
- surplus research after all useful nodes;
- doctrine discount effect;
- research acceleration from population growth;
- Scourge impact on research generation.

## 9.2 Aptitude pacing

Measure:

- milestone completion timing;
- points available at hour 1, 3, midgame and endgame;
- percentage of a discipline reachable per run;
- unused points at ending;
- dominant aptitude choices;
- whether milestones push repetitive behaviour.

## 9.3 Distinctness test

For every technology and aptitude pair, ask:

- Are these granting the same benefit through different currencies?
- Would the game lose a meaningful distinction if one were removed?
- Does the aptitude express player identity?
- Does the technology express network capability?

Merge or redesign weak duplicates.

# 10. Save and migration

Likely changes:

- expanded milestone progress/state;
- aptitude exclusions or respec history;
- technology era/tags are definition data and need not persist;
- doctrine/stance fields remain compatible;
- optional progression recommendation state should remain derived.

Migration rules:

- preserve all owned technologies and aptitudes;
- map renamed/merged nodes explicitly;
- refund aptitude points for removed nodes;
- do not silently revoke capabilities from old saves;
- rebuild milestone progress from cumulative stats where possible;
- retain completion timestamps where already stored;
- version any semantic change to purchase/exclusion behavior.

# 11. Implementation phases

## Phase 0 -- audit

- inventory every technology, aptitude and milestone;
- classify each by outcome, branch/discipline and player journey era;
- identify duplicates, filler and unclear effects;
- record current pacing from fixed saves/bot runs;
- make no balance changes yet.

## Phase 1 -- progression report and data cleanup

- add canonical outcome/era/category fields;
- add milestone progress functions;
- add DOM-free progression report;
- add definition validators;
- update tests.

## Phase 2 -- Development surface

- remove compact mini tree;
- add one launcher with both currencies;
- create responsive full Development surface;
- move Research, Aptitudes, Milestones and Identity into clear tabs;
- preserve all existing purchase actions.

## Phase 3 -- milestone expansion

- add cross-style milestone families;
- add progress and recent completion presentation;
- add Chronicle/title hooks;
- rebalance point cadence.

## Phase 4 -- strip and consolidate content

- merge or remove weak research nodes;
- redesign passive aptitude filler;
- clarify doctrine placement;
- add respec policy;
- migrate saves explicitly.

## Phase 5 -- evidence and tuning

- bot/manual pacing curves;
- choice-frequency report;
- narrow/wide responsive review;
- keyboard and screen-reader pass;
- update `SPEC.md` status only after evidence.

# 12. Dependency-free tests

Required checks:

1. every tech prerequisite references a real node;
2. tech graph has no unintended cycles;
3. mutual exclusions are symmetric or explicitly one-way;
4. every visible node has a player-facing outcome and UI home;
5. every aptitude has valid discipline, prerequisite and effect reference;
6. every milestone progress function returns finite bounded values;
7. milestones grant rewards exactly once;
8. repeated buy/sell or route creation cannot farm milestone points;
9. doctrine remains one-per-run;
10. respec preserves point totals and journal history;
11. old saves preserve owned capabilities;
12. removed/merged aptitude migration refunds correctly;
13. progression report does not mutate state;
14. same state produces identical recommendations;
15. compact launcher shows both currencies and pending choices;
16. mini tech canvas is absent after migration;
17. full Development surface renders at narrow and wide dimensions;
18. every research and aptitude control is keyboard reachable;
19. text does not wrap into overlapping controls under browser-boot fixture sizes;
20. disabled choices explain why;
21. no dependency, package or build step is introduced.

---

# 13. Acceptance criteria

This overhaul is complete when:

- Research, Aptitudes, Milestones, Doctrine and Identity have one coherent home;
- research and aptitude points remain visibly and mechanically distinct;
- the compact tech tree is replaced by a minimal launcher;
- the full research tree has one primary responsive representation;
- aptitude cards no longer depend on cramped inline wrapping;
- milestones are numerous, varied, visible in progress and resistant to farming;
- research unlocks network capability while aptitudes express player mastery;
- doctrine feels like a run-defining commitment rather than an ordinary node;
- every progression choice explains its effect and UI destination;
- weak duplicate/filler nodes are merged, moved or removed;
- save migration preserves earned capability and refunds removed choices;
- pacing is supported by evidence rather than intuition;
- all work remains deterministic, accessible, zero dependency and directly playable.

## Final principle

Research should describe what civilisation has made possible. Aptitudes should describe what the player has learned to do with it. Milestones should prove that the distinction was earned.
