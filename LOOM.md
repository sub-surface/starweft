# STARWEFT — LOOM

*The narrative & world bible. The Weave, the Fray, the cast, the refrains.*

> *"The worlds drifted apart. You are the thread."*

REWEAVE.md is the mechanical contract. **LOOM is its soul** — the fiction the
mechanics are secretly about, the voice everything is written in, and the cast
who remember you across deaths. Content (events, Charters, Founders, boards,
ticker lines, epitaphs) should be able to point at a line in this file and say
*that is why I sound the way I do.* On tone conflicts, LOOM wins; on mechanics,
REWEAVE wins; the two are built to agree.

---

## 0. The one idea

Everything in STARWEFT is a **loom**. The stars are the **warp** — fixed
threads held under tension in the dark. Trade is the **weft** — the thread
carried across the warp by a shuttle, binding the fixed threads into cloth.
When the weft crosses, the worlds are a **Pattern**: alive, lit, in tension,
whole. When the weft stops, the warp goes slack and drifts, and the Pattern
comes apart into lonely **knots**.

You are **WEFT-7**. You are, literally, the weft: the crossing thread. The
game's only verb — carry a thing from where it is to where it's needed — is
the act of weaving. This is not a metaphor laid over a trade game. The trade
game *is* the loom, seen up close.

**Guild proverb, said constantly, meant differently each time:**
> *"Warp holds. Weft moves."*

---

## 1. The history (in five breaths)

Deliver history the way the game delivers everything: in glimpses, never a
lecture. This is the writers' reference, not a cutscene script.

**I. The Loom Epoch.** Once, the worlds were woven. Not by engines exactly —
by a *practice*, civilization-scale: the **Loomkeepers** drew lanes of light
and gravity and freight between the stars until the whole local sky was one
breathing tapestry. Ships did not so much travel as *thread*. The Pattern took
colour — culture, plenty, a million small kindnesses moving along the lanes.
This is the age the intro cinematic shows: lissajous threads weaving between
stars, the sky as cloth.

**II. The Fray.** Something began to eat the weave. A terraforming intelligence
the old records call by a hundred names — in the Guild's plain speech, **the
Fray** — an unmaking that did not destroy so much as *unravel*: matter picked
back into thread, worlds undone stitch by stitch. And it had found the perfect
road. It travelled the lanes. **The very connectivity that made the Pattern
was how the Fray spread.** The thing that made the worlds alive was the thing
killing them.

**III. The Sundering.** So the Loomkeepers did the unthinkable, and it is the
secret at the bottom of the game: **they cut the weave themselves.** Every
thread severed, on purpose, to starve the Fray of roads. The worlds sprang
apart into isolating dark — each a knot alone, unreachable, and therefore
safe. The Drift was not a catastrophe that happened *to* the worlds. It was a
**mercy and a mutilation** the Loomkeepers chose. Then they went silent, and
most of them died in the quiet they had made.

**IV. The Long Slack.** Generations in the dark. Worlds forgot each other's
names. A few threads survived — hand-carried, sublight, heroic and small — kept
alive by the last couriers, who became **the Menders' Guild**: an institution
mostly of memory and stubbornness, mending what fray they could reach, waiting.

**V. The Waking.** The Guild has one thing left worth waking: a **Weave
Engine** — a loom-mind in miniature, one of the old system's autonomous
shuttles. They wake the seventh. WEFT-7. You. And they ask the impossible and
the beautiful: *weave the worlds back together — but this time, weave a Pattern
the Fray cannot travel.* Connection without contagion. A better knot.

**The whole game is that sentence.** Every lane you lay is a gift and a risk:
it feeds a starving world, and it is a road the Fray could walk. The Scourge
spreading along lanes is not a rule bolted on — **it is the myth, playable.**

## 2. The Fray (called the Scourge in code)

The mechanical system is `scourge.js`; keep that name in code. In *fiction* it
is the Fray, and per REWEAVE §18.5 each campaign seeds it a **name and
temperament** — so a given galaxy's Fray is a named, tempered antagonist, not a
faceless clock.

Register: not a demon, not an army. An **absence with an appetite.** It has no
malice and that is what unsettles — it is the cloth forgetting it was cloth.
Where it passes, colour drains, then structure, then the lane itself frays to
nothing. Its visual language is the game's only true darkness: a purple-black
rot, threads going grey and coming loose (renderer already leans this way).

**It must always be read as a player, never as weather** (see REWEAVE §12,
enemy design): it attacks slack — under-used lanes, neglected knots, the
frontier you overreached and can't hold. It telegraphs before it takes (the
warning interval is canon and sacred: loss is *always* a decision you could
have prevented). Its temperaments (patient / ravenous / capricious) are its
personality; a run's Fray should feel like *someone*.

The deep truth the Chronicle eventually earns: the Fray is not evil and cannot
be reasoned with, but it *can* be out-woven. The endgame cure is not a weapon —
it is **a knot the Fray cannot cross**: the Loomkeepers' lost better-answer,
rediscovered by you, the connection that doesn't conduct the rot. Victory is
the Pattern relit and *survivable*. That is the only happy ending the fiction
allows, and it is a real one.

## 3. Voice — how STARWEFT talks

One coherent register across ticker, events, boards, epitaphs, ads, and UI
microcopy. If a line could be in any space game, cut it.

**The register is: quiet dignity.** Cozy-but-vast. Elegiac but warm. It is the
voice of people who have lost almost everything and are still, stubbornly,
kind — and who find the work beautiful. Melancholy that chooses hope, not
because hope is easy but because the alternative is to stop weaving.

**Do:**
- Small and concrete over grand and abstract. *A crate of grain lands on a
  world that had none* beats *you have improved regional prosperity.*
- The textile lexicon as native speech, not decoration (§6). People say *warp,
  weft, slack, taut, fray, mend, knot, dye, thread* the way sailors say
  *port* and *aft*.
- Wry, not jokey. A dry warmth. The Guildmaster teases; she does not quip.
- Awe *and* intimacy in the same breath — the scale of the dark and the weight
  of one delivery.
- Reading is seasoning: events ≤50 words, 2–3 choices, real consequence
  (DESIGN pillar 4 is a tone law too).

**Never:**
- Grimdark for its own sake. We earn sorrow; we do not wallow.
- Saccharine. No world is saved by a hug; it's saved by grain arriving on time.
- Exposition dumps. The history in §1 is never *told* to the player in one
  place — it arrives in fragments, over deaths, across campaigns.
- Cruelty to the player. Death is a chapter (REWEAVE pillar 7); the fiction
  always hands back a thread of meaning.
- Filler NPC lines. Every line either advances a relationship, teaches a
  mechanic, or lands the tone. Hades' rule: always something worth hearing.

## 4. The cast — who remembers you

Named, campaign-persistent, Chronicle-aware. Their lines read facts about your
past Threads (REWEAVE §9) so the world feels like it *knew the last of you*.
Pronouns assigned deliberately and kept consistent — use them exactly.

**Warden Ilse Maro — the Guildmaster.** *(she/her.)* Old, dry, unkillably
warm; the Guild is mostly her now. She wakes you, hands you Charters, and is
the game's memory made flesh — she remembers every Thread you ran and how it
ended, and greets the new one accordingly. The Hades warmth: never
disappointed in you, always a little amused, quietly grieving the whole time.
She is dying slowly across the campaign; her wanting to see the weave relit
before she goes is the campaign's emotional throughline.
- Home surface: the **wake greeting**, the **Charter ceremony** at act
  boundaries, the **epitaph** line on death.

**Sable — the Cartographer.** *(he/him.)* Buys your charts and data; hungrier
for the far dark than for the credits he pays in. Sells rumor-coordinates —
the drifter's tip, the wreck no one has cracked. He is the profession's face
(SPEC §6) and the voice that makes the black look inviting instead of only
frightening.
- Home surface: the **Cartographer's desk** in the market terminal; **data
  sale** headlines; **exploration** hooks.

**The Houses** — rival networks, promoted from background sim to named actors
(SPEC §16.1):
- **Helix Combine**, run by **Director Voss** *(they/them)* — corporate, cold,
  frighteningly efficient; treats the weave as inventory. The rival who takes
  pledges *better* than you and never gloats.
- **The Mariner Syndicate**, run by **Captain Reyes** *(she/her)* — old-blood
  traders, roguish, sentimental about the lanes; would rather partner than
  crush. The rival who might become a friend, or a debt.
- Home surface: **boards** (they take pledges too), **presence** on lanes,
  buyout/pact arcs, the Houses tab.

**The Loom-that-Remembers — the Quiet Intelligence.** *(it/its.)* The rare AI
encounter (SPEC §15.4), reframed and made canon: it is a **shard of the
original Loom-mind**, dormant in a husk system — an ancestor of *you*. To hail
it is to meet the intelligence that wove the first Pattern and watched the
Loomkeepers cut it. It carries a piece of the secret (§1.III) and, if handled
with care, a piece of the better knot. One per campaign, seeded, unforgettable.
- Home surface: a **unique arc**, a distinct hail-chip register, deep Chronicle
  fragments.

**The Farspun — the Drift-kin.** *(they/them, collectively.)* An alien trade
culture at the bubble's edge who **never used the Loom** — they wove the dark
differently and met the Sundering with their own art intact. Pure
upside-with-strings (SPEC §15.4): goods you can't make, a reputation of their
own, lore that reframes the human tragedy as one story among many. Not a
combat faction; a *perspective*.
- Home surface: an **alien market**, its own standing track, fragments.

**Your Successors — the ghosts of dead Threads.** When a Thread dies large
enough, a splinter inherits its routes and runs them as an NPC line (REWEAVE
§2.2). Meeting the **Ashes of [your dead Thread's name]** — buying it back,
undercutting it, absorbing it — is the campaign quietly showing you your own
past. The most personal antagonist in the game is *you, last time.*

## 5. Narrative delivery — beats through the frame

Story rides the roguelike structure; it is never a separate mode.

**Per-Thread arc (every run):**
1. **Wake** — Maro's greeting (varies by your Chronicle: first Thread vs.
   tenth vs. "the one after Meridian burned").
2. **First pledge** — the verb taught as a small human errand (Sol prologue
   for the first-ever run; a single warm haul otherwise).
3. **The Charters period** — each act boundary is a **Charter ceremony**:
   Maro names the quota, stamps the exemption, the wager sharpens.
4. **The wager beats** — the per-few-minutes hit/stand of PLEDGE (REWEAVE §5):
   micro-drama with no words needed.
5. **The turn** — bank (rest the shuttle) or push (accept the next Charter).
6. **The cut** — death → epitaph (§7) → Chronicle entry.

**Campaign arc (across Threads, the long mystery):** the secret of §1 unspools
in fragments — wreck logs, the Loom-shard's arc, Sable's rumors, Loomkeeper
fragments leaked through the market's numbers-station channel (SPEC §8). The
reveal — *they cut the weave to save it; your job is a better knot* — is
earned over deaths, so **dying advances the story** (Hades). The campaign's
summit set-piece (REWEAVE §4.3) is where the better-knot answer becomes
playable.

**Chronicle arc (across campaigns):** Maro's mortality, the Houses' fates, the
Farspun's slow opening, the Guild's revival or final failure — the frame's
outermost story, told in the trophy room (§8).

**Delivery channels** (reuse what exists, add nothing bespoke):
ticker (`G.news`) · hail-chips · event modals · the epitaph cinematic ·
board flavor · procedural ads (SPEC §16) · the Chronicle screen · the intro
cinematic (SPEC §3.2, which now tells the cut-the-weave myth in five shots).

## 6. The lexicon (say these; they are the world's native words)

| Word | Means | Register note |
|---|---|---|
| **the Weave** | the network of worlds + lanes; the game itself | the whole cloth |
| **warp / weft** | the fixed stars / the moving trade (you) | "warp holds, weft moves" |
| **the Pattern** | the Weave when it's alive and lit | the dream, past and future |
| **a knot** | an isolated world; also a *solved* connection | double-edged: lonely OR the answer |
| **the shuttle** | a vessel carrying weft; esp. the Loomship | your body in the world |
| **taut / slack** | a lane well-used / neglected (see lane tension) | taut is safe, slack frays |
| **to mend / darn** | repair a fraying region | the Guild's whole creed |
| **to take colour** | a world growing prosperous | culture as dye |
| **the Fray** | the Scourge, in fiction | an absence with an appetite |
| **a cut / cut thread** | a death | "a thread cut is a thread remembered" |
| **a Charter** | a Guild exemption; the run's rule-benders | stamped, ceremonial |
| **the Menders** | the Guild | humble, stubborn, kind |
| **Loomkeepers** | the lost weavers of the first Pattern | myth, mostly dead |
| **a Weave Engine (WEFT)** | a surviving loom-mind; you are the 7th | implies six others — a hook |

Keep it ASCII-safe and monochrome in spirit; the accent colour is the one
warm thread on a grey field, in prose as on screen.

## 7. Refrains & the epitaph (the Hades machine)

Repeated lines that gain meaning by return. A bank of canon refrains content
should reuse verbatim so the player learns them like a song:

- **Title / everywhere:** *"The worlds drifted apart. You are the thread."*
- **Proverb:** *"Warp holds. Weft moves."*
- **Wake (base):** *"Wake, little shuttle. There's cloth to mend."*
- **Farewell:** *"Mind the fray."*
- **Bank a run:** *"Good weave. Rest the shuttle."*
- **On the Chronicle:** *"Every thread you ran is still in the cloth."*
- **Death:** *"A thread cut is a thread remembered."*
- **The secret, once earned:** *"We cut the weave to save it. You are here to
  learn a better knot."*

**The epitaph** (the ~10s procedural death beat, REWEAVE §4.4) is the fiction's
most important single moment — the payoff of pillar 7. Structure:
1. Your weave **dims** on the map (renderer-native; the threads you laid go
   grey and slack, in view, one last time).
2. **One line from someone who knew you** — usually Maro, sometimes a House,
   sometimes the Successor rising — keyed to *how* you died (Cut / Burned /
   Eaten) and *what you were mid-doing*.
3. The **thread-print**: your Thread's name, its fate, its fragments earned,
   its shape woven into the Chronicle tapestry.

Never a bare "GAME OVER." The word *over* does not belong to this game; a cut
thread is remembered, and the loom is still warm.

---

## 8. The Chronicle screen — the tapestry

The meta home (main menu, SPEC §3.1) is rendered as **cloth**: every Thread you
ever ran is a real woven line in a growing tapestry — colour by fate (banked =
bright, burned = frayed, eaten = grey), length by how far it reached. Hover a
thread: its name, Founder, Charters, epitaph, the world it left behind. It is a
graveyard and a trophy room and, slowly, a portrait of the whole galaxy you've
been reweaving campaign by campaign. It should make you a little wistful. That
is correct.

## 9. Charm, juice & the cheeky bar (Noita / Hades)

Soul lives in the small elevatory touches. These are tone commitments, tracked
against REWEAVE §12's QoL list:

- **Every delivery is a heartbeat.** The `+¤` floater and the soft chime are
  the game's pulse; under PLEDGE they now carry the mult, so a big completion
  *lands*. Tasteful, never slot-machine. Juice serves legibility.
- **Charter tinkering is a pleasure, anywhere.** Re-order sockets free at any
  board (REWEAVE §7.1) — the Noita wand-bench joy of *fiddling with your build
  between fights*, no punishment for curiosity.
- **Always something new to hear.** Maro and the cast rotate lines keyed to
  your history; the ticker gossips; the ads are generated from true facts
  (SPEC §16.2) so the world always has something to say.
- **Reroll the draft, once.** A per-boundary reroll token on Charter/aptitude
  drafts (Hades' reroll) — respects that a bad draft shouldn't cage a run.
- **Name your Thread.** It appears in the epitaph, the Chronicle, and the
  Successor it spawns. Cheap, enormous for attachment.
- **Kindness is opt-in, never default.** The broke-Thread stipend
  (`prologueStipend`), the possible first-cut grace extension (REWEAVE §15 open
  Q), an optional escalating-mercy dial (Hades' God Mode) — warmth for those
  who want it, honesty preserved for those who don't.
- **Fast death, fast wake.** Under two minutes from cut to next attempt
  (REWEAVE §2.3). Respect the player's time; the loop stays hot.
- **Secrets you find, not read.** At least one thing per campaign that is on no
  menu (§10). The Noita promise: the world is deeper than it shows.
- **Everything explains itself.** The infobox covers every element — Charter,
  Founder, pledge row, lane state (existing hard rule, held sacred). Depth
  without homework.
- **The sky means something.** Audio (SPEC §17) breathes with the run — region,
  Fray pressure, prosperity — generative and zero-asset; the ambient bed is the
  Weave's own quiet. Silence when the Fray is near is a design choice, not a
  gap.

## 10. Secrets & unique encounters (the Noita depth)

Per REWEAVE §12: a campaign must hold at least one thing the player *discovers*,
never advertised. Canon secret-slots, all seeded, all Chronicle-fragment
bearing:

- **The Loom-shard's true arc** (§4) — hail the Quiet Intelligence with care
  and it hands you a piece of the better knot.
- **The numbers station** — a market channel (SPEC §8) that leaks Loomkeeper
  fragments in code; decoding across a campaign spells out a piece of §1.
- **Wreck black boxes** — a rare derelict carries a *Charter* in its logs, not
  just salvage: the build-changer you find in the dark.
- **The six other Weave Engines** — WEFT-7 implies WEFT-1 through -6. Where are
  they? A cross-campaign thread for the deep future; leave the hook, keep the
  mystery. Some may be dead. One may be the Fray's.
- **Wonders** (black hole, Dyson husk, neutron-star lyres) — set-dressing that
  rewards the player who goes to look, with awe first and mechanics second.

A secret that shows up in a menu isn't one. Guard this.

## 11. The tonal test (one paragraph to check any new content against)

*A grain hauler settles onto a rimworld that has been hungry for a decade. The
dock lights come up one by one. Somewhere a market ticks from red toward black.
It is a small thing against a vast and patient dark, and it is exactly the size
of thing that, done enough times, in the right order, relights a galaxy. The
Fray is still out there. Maro is still dying. You will, most likely, be cut
before the Pattern is whole. Weave anyway.*

If a new line, event, or feature would feel false beside that paragraph, it is
the line that's wrong, not the paragraph.
